We replace the traditional putty impression with an intraoral scan that creates a
precise digital model of the mouth. The lab designs the prosthesis with CAD
software and manufactures it with a milling machine or 3D printing.

The result is a more precise, more comfortable prosthesis with a better aesthetic
fit, and fewer try-in and adjustment visits than with the traditional workflow.

## Types of prosthesis

Prostheses are classified by what supports them,
[on natural teeth](/en/tratamientos/protesis-sobre-dientes/) or
[on implants](/en/tratamientos/protesis-sobre-implantes/), and by material,
porcelain or resin. They can also be:

- **Fixed**: cemented or screwed in, and not removed by the patient. These include
  crowns, bridges and full prostheses on
  [implants](/en/especialidades/implantes-dentales/).
- **Removable**: the patient can put them in and take them out for cleaning. Usually
  indicated when several teeth are missing and a more affordable solution is
  wanted, or when the condition of the bone doesn't yet allow other options.

The choice depends on the specific needs of each case, which we assess at the first
visit. When the main goal is improving the look of teeth that are still present,
without replacing missing ones, the alternative is usually a
[dental veneer](/en/especialidades/estetica-dental/) rather than a prosthesis.

## Caring for your prosthesis

- **Fixed**: cared for like natural teeth, with brushing and floss or interdental
  brushes, paying special attention to where the prosthesis meets the gum.
- **Removable**: removed for daily cleaning with a specific brush, and stored in
  water or a cleaning solution when not being worn.

In both cases, regular check-ups allow any misfit to be caught early, before it
affects the teeth or implants providing support.
