Dental trauma care covers the diagnosis and treatment of injuries to teeth, gums and
bone caused by knocks or accidents. It's a common situation in sport, especially in
contact disciplines, hockey, cycling, skating or outdoor activities.

A trauma can cause anything from a small crack to a fracture or the complete loss of
a tooth, as well as nerve damage that requires
[root canal treatment](/en/especialidades/endodoncia/). Time is critical: acting
quickly can make the difference between saving the tooth and losing it.

## Dental emergencies

We provide our patients with a dental emergency phone line, +34 619 186 579, to
respond immediately to any trauma. We recommend coming to the clinic as soon as
possible after any knock or impact to the mouth.

## What to do after a knock, minute by minute

- **Tooth knocked out completely**: pick it up by the crown, without touching the
  root. If it's dirty, rinse it with water or saline without rubbing it. If
  possible, put it back in its socket; if not, keep it in milk or saliva (never in
  water or dry) and come to the clinic as soon as possible.
- **Tooth loosened or displaced**: don't try to reposition it yourself. Come to the
  clinic as soon as possible to have it stabilised and the nerve assessed.
- **Fracture or chip**: keep the fragment if you find it, it may be useful for the
  reconstruction. Avoid chewing on that side until the check-up.
- **Wound to the gum or lip**: press with clean gauze to control bleeding and come to
  the clinic if it doesn't stop within a few minutes.

## Prevention in sport

For patients who play sport regularly, we recommend custom mouthguards as a
preventive measure against dental trauma. More on
[custom mouthguards](/en/tratamientos/protectores-bucales/) and on
[sports dentistry](/en/especialidades/odontologia-deportiva/).
