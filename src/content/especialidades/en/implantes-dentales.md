## What a dental implant is

A dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth. It's a fixture anchored in the
jawbone, onto which the crowns or bridges that take over the function of the missing
teeth are screwed, restoring chewing and aesthetics.

Our team uses prior digital planning to decide the position and number of fixtures
before entering the mouth, with minimally invasive techniques that guarantee precise,
safe results.

## [Single-tooth implants](/en/tratamientos/implantes-unitarios/)

When a single tooth is lost, a single-tooth implant fills that gap. Beyond the
aesthetic improvement, it's an essential treatment for keeping the mouth's structure
intact: if the tooth isn't replaced, the neighbouring teeth start shifting to fill
the empty space.

## Immediate loading: teeth in a day

The standard protocol needs about two months of bone integration before placing the
final tooth. If you'd rather not wait, we remove the damaged teeth and place the
implants and fixed teeth in a single day. [How immediate loading
works](/en/tratamientos/carga-inmediata/).

## [Full-arch implants](/en/tratamientos/implantes-arcada-completa/)

Full-arch restorations don't need one implant per missing tooth. Success depends on
a detailed prior study: we calculate the optimal number and placement of fixtures so
the [prosthesis](/en/especialidades/protesis-dental/) is well balanced and loads are
distributed correctly.

## Bone regeneration

When there isn't enough bone to give the implant stability, we apply bone grafting
techniques before surgery within our [oral surgery](/en/especialidades/cirugia-oral/)
area, also digitally planned. As an alternative to grafting in the back of the upper
jaw, we consider [pterygoid implants](/en/tratamientos/implantes-pterigoideos/).

## Gum health before the implant

Healthy gums are just as important as the bone: untreated periodontitis is one of
the main causes of long-term implant failure. If you have a history of gum disease,
we start by stabilising it in [periodontics](/en/especialidades/periodoncia/) before
planning the implant.
